# Madagascar Agricultural Stakeholders Research Summary

## Key Insights (≤ 500 words)

Madagascar's agricultural ecosystem reveals a sector in dynamic transformation, characterized by strong traditional foundations and emerging digital innovation. The research identified 45 key stakeholders across the entire value chain, with several notable trends:

**Government Leadership & Policy Innovation:** The Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MINAE) demonstrates proactive leadership through programs like "Diaben'ny famokarana" and active participation in international forums like UNFSS+4 and SADC. The ministry's recent "Pack Riz 2025" initiative shows commitment to food security and sustainable agriculture.

**Research Excellence & International Collaboration:** FOFIFA and CIRAD provide world-class agricultural research capabilities, with strong partnerships spanning decades. The University of Antananarivo's School of Agronomy (ESSA) educates 1100+ students across 6 specializations, creating a solid foundation for agricultural innovation.

**Digital Transformation Emerging:** The sector shows early signs of digital transformation with FATAPLUS B2B marketplace (2020), Agritech Madagascar Association (2023), and a landmark $90M Israeli smart agriculture partnership (2025). Government support through MINAE-MNDPT partnerships indicates commitment to agricultural technology adoption.

**Strong International Support Network:** UN agencies (FAO, WFP), development banks (AFD), and international NGOs (CRS, Aga Khan Network) provide substantial support, creating a robust development ecosystem. This international presence facilitates knowledge transfer and capacity building.

**Diverse Private Sector Growth:** The identification of 289+ registered agricultural companies indicates a vibrant private sector, though the research captured 25 major players across processing, equipment, inputs, and specialized services. Companies like SOMECA (1955) and Compagnie Madecasse (2006) demonstrate long-term commitment and market expertise.

**Regional Disparities & Opportunities:** While national institutions are well-represented, regional coverage shows gaps, with only 8 organizations identified outside Antananarivo. This presents opportunities for strengthening rural agricultural service providers and expanding value chain integration.

**Sustainability Focus:** Organizations like CIRAD (agroecology research), IDEMCO (sustainable agribusiness consulting), and Aga Khan Network (environmental protection) demonstrate growing emphasis on sustainable agricultural practices.

**Key Gaps Identified:** Limited agritech startups (3 identified), regional concentration in Antananarivo, limited agricultural finance institutions, absence of certification bodies, and minimal venture capital presence represent opportunities for sector development.

The research reveals Madagascar's agricultural sector as a dynamic ecosystem with strong foundations and emerging innovation potential, requiring targeted interventions to address regional disparities and accelerate digital transformation while maintaining sustainability focus.
